A little country. A lot of dog.

The Farmhouse has been part of the family for generations.

It was Jamie’s grandparents’ home, back when the Ells family was farming everything from cattle and apples to poultry. These days, it has a very different crowd coming through the door.

How we got here

Back to dogs, right here at home.

I worked in dog boarding before taking a break for about ten years while I worked on a dairy farm. I missed working with dogs, even though dogs themselves were never exactly missing from my life.

We have five of our own now, and at one point we had seven. So when the idea of working with dogs again started taking shape, doing it here at home felt pretty natural.

Jamie, Weston, and I have a cow/calf farm with about 90 cows. The dogs are never in with the cattle, but they can see them from the fenced yard. When you pull in the driveway, there is no mistaking that you have arrived at a farm.

The house itself is big, old, and full of history. It once belonged to Jamie’s grandparents, and a lot of local people know the Ells name from years of farming in the area.

There were cattle, apple orchards, turkey quota, chicken quota, and plenty happening here long before Farmhouse & Fields came along.

Now the Farmhouse has a new job. During the day, it belongs to the dogs.

They have the house, the fenced yard, lots of outdoor time, familiar faces, and a place where they can play hard and still settle in when they need a break.
